E. Deniz Gedikli

Associate Professor

Ocean and Resources Engineering · University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa

I study how ocean flows, structures, and marine environments interact. My work combines laboratory experiments, numerical modeling, and data-driven methods to understand fluid–structure interactions and improve the design and operation of ocean systems.

My research spans flow-induced vibration, marine renewable energy, hydroelasticity, and Arctic engineering. I direct the Fluid-Structure Interactions and Nonlinear Dynamics Laboratory at UH Mānoa and am an affiliate of the Hawaiʻi Marine Energy Center.

What I work on

Research interests

Questions at the intersection of fluid mechanics, structural dynamics, and ocean engineering.

01

Flow-induced vibration

Experiments and models of vortex-induced vibration, wake dynamics, and active control of flexible structures in flow.

02

Marine renewable energy

Hydrodynamics and fluid–structure interactions in wave energy converters, including oscillating water columns and coupled systems.

03

Hydroelasticity and wave–ice interactions

How waves deform ice and flexible marine structures, and how those responses shape environmental loads and operations.

04

Data-driven ocean engineering

Reduced-order models, nonlinear time-series methods, and sensing approaches for complex fluid–structure systems.
